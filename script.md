# PART 4: JUDGE CROSS-EXAMINATION & Q&A PLAYBOOK

#### Q1: "Is this medically accredited? What if a wrong AI recommendation kills someone?"
* **Answer by Sharav (Grade 4):**
  > "Respected Judge, our app does not guess or hallucinate medical advice. Every protocol is strictly built on the **American Heart Association 2020 Guidelines** and the **AIIMS Emergency Medicine Standard Operating Procedures**.  
  > Furthermore, whenever our system detects a dangerous red flag—like a person not breathing or spurting arterial blood—it immediately locks the conversational chat and displays an emergency **112 calling button**. It is a pre-hospital triage bridge for the first five minutes, not a replacement for a doctor."

#### Q2: "What happens if there is zero internet connectivity during a disaster or inside a concrete basement?"
* **Answer by Samyak:**
  > "Sanjeevani is architected as an Offline-First Progressive Web App (PWA) using Service Workers and local cache. All core protocols—CPR, choking, severe bleeding, burns, and snakebites—are saved directly into the browser storage on first load. If the network drops or times out beyond 10 seconds, it automatically runs 100% locally with zero internet needed."

#### Q3: "Why would bystanders actually scan a QR code instead of just taking photos or running away?"
* **Answer by Ishanvi (Grade 4):**
  > "Social science proves that people in a crowd freeze not because they don't care, but because nobody knows who is responsible! When someone shouts *'Someone help!'*, nobody moves.  
  > But when our rescuer points the QR code and says, *'You in the blue shirt, scan this and call 112; you in the red jacket, scan this and find an AED,'* the confusion vanishes! They scan it with any phone camera—no app to install—and get one single, unambiguous task with a countdown timer. That breaks crowd paralysis and turns onlookers into a rescue team."

#### Q4: "How is Jan Aushadhi generic Augmentin 625 really ₹55 compared to ₹210 for GSK's Augmentin? Is the quality identical?"
* **Answer by Sharav (Grade 4):**
  > "Yes, the quality is 100% bio-equivalent! Under the government's **Jan Aushadhi scheme**, medicines are bought in bulk from WHO-GMP certified factories and tested in NABL-accredited laboratories.  
  > The ₹155 price difference is not because of lower quality; it is because generic medicines eliminate expensive TV ads, sales commissions, fancy packaging, and brand markups. The active medicine—Amoxicillin and Potassium Clavulanate—is exactly the same."

#### Q5: "Why do you emphasize 110 BPM and 5 to 6 cm depth specifically for CPR? Why not faster or deeper?"
* **Answer by Sharav (Grade 4):**
  > "Because that is the physiological sweet spot tested by the American Heart Association!  
  > If you press faster than 120 beats per minute, the heart doesn't have enough time to refill with blood between pushes. If you press slower than 100, not enough blood reaches the brain. 110 is right in the middle!  
  > And for depth: pressing shallower than 5 centimeters doesn't squeeze the heart enough; pressing deeper than 6 centimeters can crack ribs and cause chest injuries."

#### Q6: "Why is Aspirin contraindicated for patients under 16? Why can't a teenager with chest pain take it?"
* **Answer by Sharav (Grade 4):**
  > "Giving Aspirin to children and teenagers under 16 during or after a viral fever can trigger **Reye’s Syndrome**. It is very dangerous—it causes liver failure and brain swelling, with an over 30% death rate! Doctors in India, the US, and the UK have banned Aspirin for kids under 16. Sanjeevani immediately warns users and recommends Paracetamol or physical cooling instead."

#### Q7: "What is an SBAR handover docket and why do paramedics care about it?"
* **Answer by Ishanvi (Grade 4):**
  > "**SBAR** stands for **Situation, Background, Assessment, and Recommendation**. It is the international medical communication standard recommended by the WHO and AIIMS.  
  > Studies show that over 70% of pre-hospital mistakes happen because people shout confusing stories when an ambulance arrives. Our SBAR QR code transfers a clean, timestamped digital record in under half a second directly to the paramedic’s device!"

#### Q8: "Why didn't you build this as an Android/iOS native app from the Play Store?"
* **Answer by Samyak:**
  > "Because in the first 300 seconds, an app store download is a dangerous delay. Asking a panicked bystander to download a 100MB app, accept permissions, and type an OTP burns 3 to 5 minutes of oxygenated brain time. A web app opens in any browser in under 2 seconds with zero installation."

#### Q9: "If a rescuer is panicking, won't waiting 1.5 seconds per card frustrate them?"
* **Answer by Ishanvi (Grade 4):**
  > "When people are terrified, dumping a wall of six medical paragraphs causes cognitive freezing and skipped steps. The 1.5-second break matches human reading speed under adrenaline.  
  > But if an experienced rescuer wants to see everything immediately, we gave them a **⚡ Show All Steps** button at the top that opens all cards instantly with a single tap!"

| **300 Seconds** | 5 minutes. The biological golden window before brain cells die without oxygen. | High-energy ATP depletes in 2–3 min; irreversible cerebral ischemia begins at 4 min (240s). |
